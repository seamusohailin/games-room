Daniel's Island Chart

Gran says: "The islands are small, Daniel, but no two of them are quite alike."

In your lesson, Daniel travels the Caribbean with his grandmother. Now he is drawing a sea chart for his cousin, and he wants it to answer one question:

Why isn't every Caribbean island the same?

There are five parts, and each one adds to the chart. Start with Part 1, then do the others in any order. At the end, you'll write a letter back to Daniel's cousin.

You can stop whenever you like: the chart is saved on this device. Daniel and his grandmother come from the story in your lesson. They're made up, but the places are real.

    2. Northern Australia gets the same kind of storm, but there it's called a cyclone. Use Gran's rule, late summer and autumn, but for our half of the world. Which months would you predict?
      Australia's cyclone season runs from November to April. How close did Gran's rule get you? Which months did it miss?
    3. One question you'd like to find out about the Caribbean:

    Why isn't every Caribbean island the same?

    How it was made. Some are mountainous volcanic islands, with misty peaks, short rushing rivers and waterfalls. Others are flat coral cays that get their fresh water from rain and from water held underground.
    How big it is. The four Greater Antilles (Cuba, Hispaniola, Jamaica and Puerto Rico) are large. The Lesser Antilles are small, and curve away to the south-east.
    Who settled it. Spanish settlers built Cuba's old plazas, so Cuba and the Dominican Republic speak Spanish. Jamaica speaks English, and Haiti speaks French and Creole. African, European and Indigenous peoples blended to make the islands' music, food and festivals.
    What they share. Warm weather all year, a dry season from December to April, a wet season from May to November, and hurricanes in late summer and autumn. That's August to November up there, with September usually the busiest.
